# Alex — Agency Owner and Reporting Reseller

Preliminary product persona (proto); Alex is a fictional identity, not a researched biography of Joel Widman. Prepared 2026-09-23.

## Role

Product scope: an agency owner considering Metric Flow both for internal performance reporting and resale to existing clients. The PM selected this role and identified Joel Widman of Anderson The Fish as the reference customer [P1]. These are PM-provided project facts, not independently validated behavioral findings. The Wix marketplace profile corroborates an established client-service business, but does not establish Joel's reporting habits [S1].

## Goals

PM-stated goals: sell Metric Flow to existing customers and understand the agency's own performance [P1]. Actual buying intent, pricing and adoption remain unvalidated.

Research-backed category themes:

- Understand financial performance beyond revenue alone, including where projects or services contribute to profitability. An agency's own account, an agency-owner interview summary and an agency finance practitioner independently support this theme [S2, S3, S4].
- Make client reporting useful for interpreting performance and deciding what to do next, rather than only displaying metrics. Three reporting publishers support this theme, including attributed agency practitioner comments; all have commercial interests [S5, S6, S7].

## Pain Points

Category evidence identifies incomplete financial visibility as an obstacle to agency decisions about pricing, resources and growth [S2, S3, S4]. Category reporting sources also describe effort assembling multi-source reports and difficulty translating metrics into useful client explanations [S5, S6, S7]. These are candidate pains to validate with Joel, not claims that he personally experiences them.

Joel's concrete difficulties, time spent, current workarounds and reporting failures are unknown. Workflow question 0c0e0a06-2647-44b6-b2ed-06bd82a27420 remains awaiting an answer.

## Context

This persona supports decisions about agency client-account management (story 1061), internal dashboard use (story 1071) and agency subscription plans (story 1101) [R1]. The stories establish intended product behavior, not customer research. Link this persona to agency-specific stories; it does not represent every client or every user role.

Proposed scenario for validation, not an observed workflow: Alex reviews the agency's own connected metrics, then opens an authorized client account to discuss that client's results. Separately, Alex considers offering that client paid access. The internal account, client context and commercial configuration must remain understandable throughout. This scenario derives from PM goals and existing story scope [P1, R1].

Current tools, preferred metrics, report cadence, client count, budget, technical ability and purchasing process remain unknown. Do not infer them from the agency's public profile.

## Decision Drivers

Candidate evaluation criteria inferred from the research are reliable financial visibility [S2, S3, S4] and understandable reporting connected to business decisions [S5, S6, S7]. Their relative importance to Joel requires validation.

The PM's resale goal makes configurable commercial access relevant to story 1101, but does not establish a desired price or demand for any particular branding feature [P1, R1].

Counter-evidence and limits: TapClicks describes Looker Studio's flexibility as useful for deeper custom analysis [S7]. Axelerant reports resistance to added process and concerns about accuracy [S3]. A new dashboard is therefore not automatically an improvement. Validate whether Metric Flow answers a real decision more easily than the customer's existing approach.
